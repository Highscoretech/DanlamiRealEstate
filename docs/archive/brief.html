Part one

Your brand

Every colour, every font and every edge on the website comes from this. It is the one part we cannot invent for you without seeing something first — so even a rough version of each item below lets us start.

The logo

Send the best-quality version you have. In order of preference:

  • The original design file from your designer — .ai, .svg, .psd, .eps or .cdr
  • A PNG with a transparent background
  • A light version and a dark version, if both exist
  • The icon or symbol on its own, if the logo has one

Your colours

The exact codes if you have them — hex values like #1A3D2F, or the CMYK and Pantone values from your designer.

  • If you do not have codes, just name the colours
  • Tell us any colour you specifically do not want used

Fonts and brand guide

The fonts used in your logo or existing flyers, and any brand guide or style sheet a designer prepared for you. If none exists, we will propose fonts that suit the brand.

Tagline

Any short line you use beside the business name. If you do not have one, we can write options for you to choose from.

How it should feel

One sentence is enough — luxury and exclusive, trustworthy and corporate, warm and approachable, modern and bold.

Two or three websites you like

They do not have to be real estate. Send the links and say what you like about each. This single item saves more back-and-forth than anything else in this document.

If you have no brand colours yet

This will not hold anything up. Send the logo and tell us, and we will pull the colours out of it and send you three complete palettes — each shown on a real page rather than as swatches — for you to pick one. The same applies to fonts and the tagline.

Part four

Where the information goes

Two things on this site are not just pictures and words. When somebody fills a form, that answer has to reach you and be kept somewhere you can work with. And when a video plays on the page, it is being pulled from your YouTube account. Both need something small from you.

A

Forms, and the spreadsheet behind them

There are two forms on the site: the partner registration form and the contact message box. Both work the same way underneath.

What happens when somebody presses send

  1. The visitor fills the form on danlamirealestate.com
  2. Their answers are written as a new row in your Google Sheet, instantly
  3. You get an email notification so you know somebody is waiting
  4. The visitor sees a thank-you message and receives a confirmation email
  5. You open the sheet on your phone or laptop and work the leads from there

Why a spreadsheet. No new system to learn, no password to remember, no monthly fee. You can sort it, filter it, share it with your sales team, and export it. It is the same Google Sheets you already know.

What we need from you

  • The Google account that should own the spreadsheet — a@gmail.com address, or a company Google Workspace account. Whoever owns it controls the data, so use a company account rather than a staff member’s personal one.
  • Who gets the email alerts when a form comes in. One address or several.
  • Whether partner registrations and contact messages go into one spreadsheet on separate tabs, or two separate spreadsheets.
  • Whether you also want a WhatsApp alert when somebody registers. Possible, but it adds a paid service — tell us if it matters to you.

Before you tick everything

Every extra field loses you a few registrations — people abandon long forms. A good approach is to ask for name, phone, email and how they heard about you on the website, then collect ID, bank details and next of kin later, once you have decided to work with them.

One legal note: if you do collect NIN, ID uploads or bank accounts, the site is required to have a privacy policy under the Nigeria Data Protection Act, and that data must be stored securely. We will handle the technical side. We just need you to confirm you want those fields, because they raise the obligation.

Part five

The rest

None of this stops us starting, but all of it is needed before the site goes live. Read through and answer what you can now.

Domain and email

danlamirealestate.com is already secured. Do you already have email on the domain, such as info@danlamirealestate.com? If not, tell us how many addresses you want and who they belong to, and we will set them up.

Google Business Profile

Do you have one? It is what puts your office on Google Maps when somebody searches for property in your city, and it costs nothing. If not, we will help you create it during launch.

Anything already online

An old website, a Linktree, a landing page — anything we should point at the new site or carry content across from.

Online payments

Should a buyer be able to pay a deposit on the website through Paystack or Flutterwave, or should all payments stay offline for now? This is easier to build in from the start than to add later.

Legal pages

Send your privacy policy and terms of use if they exist. If not, we will prepare standard versions for your lawyer to review. Because the site collects personal data through the forms, a privacy policy is a legal requirement, not a nicety.

Who approves

One named person who signs off designs and text. Projects slow down most when feedback arrives from several people at once and contradicts itself.

One decision that changes what we build

After launch, who adds new projects to the site — you, or us?

  • You do it. We build you a simple admin area where you log in, upload photos, type the details and publish. More build time and more cost, but you never wait on anybody to put a new estate online.
  • We do it. You send us the details and we update the site. Simpler and cheaper to build, but every change goes through us.

We need this answer early rather than late — it is much cheaper to plan for than to retrofit.

How to send everything

Put it all in one Google Drive folder and share the link — logo, photographs organised per project, documents, and a spreadsheet or document with the written answers. One folder is far easier for both of us than fifty separate messages.

Please avoid sending photographs through WhatsApp. It compresses every image it touches, and what looks sharp on your phone turns blurry across a website. Google  Drive, WeTransfer or plain email attachments keep the original quality.

Typing is not required. For any written section — your company story, a project description, what you offer investors — record a voice note instead. We will write it up properly and send it back for your approval.
